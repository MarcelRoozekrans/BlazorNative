### Placement, and why each is the same event

`v0.15.0` has no render thread; Phase 16.1 added it. At the tag, `blazornative_dispatch_event` runs the whole click synchronously on the calling thread, which is the dispatch lane: the handler, the re-render and the frame callback all run before the export returns (the `SYNCHRONOUS by contract` note on `Exports.DispatchEventCore`). Each mark is placed at the same event as on HEAD:

- **`dispatch.enter`** is the first statement of the export's `try`, as on HEAD. The export is the same function.
- **`dispatch.handler`**: HEAD marks immediately before `BlazorInterop.DispatchEventViaAccessor` in `DispatchSyncPart`, on the render thread. At the tag, the same call is in `NativeRenderer.DispatchUiEventAsync`, inside the `Dispatcher.InvokeAsync` lambda, after `BuildEventArgs`. The tag's dispatcher is the `InlineDispatcher`, so the lambda runs inline on the lane thread. The mark is immediately before that call, so it is "the handler is about to start" on both sides. What lies between `dispatch.enter` and `dispatch.handler` differs, and that is the architecture change the design expects: HEAD includes the hop from the lane to the render thread; the tag includes only the args parse, the session check and the inline dispatcher entry. 19.3's report names a shift between the two parts as an architecture difference.
- **`frame.recv`** is the entry of the shell's frame callback on both sides: the first statement of `FrameCallback.invoke`'s `try` on Android, and the first statement of `handleFrame` on iOS. At the tag, the callback for a click runs on the lane thread inside the export; on HEAD it runs on the render thread. The event is the same: the frame has reached the shell. HEAD's Android mark sits after a retired-instance check that the tag does not have, so at the tag it is simply the first statement.
- **`frame.applied`** is the end of `applyBatch` on the main thread on both sides. HEAD places it after the scroll flush and the back-state publish. The tag has no back-state publish (Phase 16.2 added it), so the scroll flush is the batch's last step and the mark follows it. On Android it is after the `try`/`finally` and after `flushScrollWires()`, so a batch that throws leaves before it and is never marked. On iOS it is in `applyBatch`'s `defer`, after `flushScrollWires()`, exactly as on HEAD: a function that cannot throw, so it runs once per batch that passed the destroyed guard.
- **`boot.content`** follows `frame.applied` in the same place, with the `bootContentPending()` short-circuit. On Android, `reportFullyDrawn` runs through the mark helper at every level, as on HEAD. No other `reportFullyDrawn` change was made.
- **`boot.start`** follows the level install on both shells, as on HEAD: `BnShellLog.setLevelFromOrdinal` in `MainActivity.onCreate`, and `BnLog.setLevelFromOrdinal` in `BnRuntime.start`.
- **`dispatch.post`** is in the shell's `onUiEvent` wiring before the lane submit, with the handler id: the `WidgetMapper(onUiEvent = ...)` lambda on Android and the `mapper.onUiEvent` closure on iOS. The tag's wiring has the same shape as HEAD's.

### The iOS .NET lines and privacy

No privacy fix was needed. The .NET lines are not redacted on the simulator: no record in the subsystem log or in the q1 evidence reads `<private>`. They are not where the design expected them, though. On iOS .NET's Console is NSLog, so a .NET line reaches the unified log in two ways: as NSLog's own record, outside the subsystem, and, when the stderr pump runs, as the pump's relay of NSLog's stderr copy, in category `native` at `Default`. The relay cannot parse NSLog's prefix, so it forwards the line as unprefixed text.

Under `xcodebuild test`, Xcode sets `OS_ACTIVITY_DT_MODE`. HEAD's pump then stands aside, so the subsystem holds no .NET line at all, while the `v0.15.0` pump has no such check. The click kind therefore reads the shell marks from the subsystem's `Perf` category and the .NET marks from the app's non-subsystem records, and never the `native` relay. The run's `dispatch-sources.ndjson` shows the result: per click run, 10 `dispatch.post` in `io.blazornative`/`Perf`, and 10 `dispatch.enter` and 10 `dispatch.handler` with no subsystem, on both sides. The collector now requires each dispatch point exactly once per click, which is also the proof that the .NET marks are readable. q1 got no .NET assertion, because a boot writes no .NET mark.

- Both collectors take `-Run <k>` to collect exactly one run.
