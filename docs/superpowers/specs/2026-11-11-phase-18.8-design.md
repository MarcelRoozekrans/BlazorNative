# Phase 18.8 Design — The generator's pipe holder (#530)

**Date:** 2026-11-11
**Phase:** `18.8`
**Surface:** Infra
**Stage:** phase (one phase, implementation-ready)
**Status:** approved 2026-11-11

## Goal

Find out which process held the reference generator's `dotnet publish` output pipe after the publish exited, and fix that at its source. The evidence comes from a controlled Windows experiment, not from inference. 17.5's bound stays and still names #530 if the hang ever returns.

## Why

The M18 Definition of Done says: "#530 is fixed. The process that outlives `dotnet publish` and holds the reference generator's output pipe is identified and fixed at its source." 17.5 shipped the likely fix: `-nodeReuse:false` plus `MSBUILDDISABLENODEREUSE=1`. Nobody has proven that it removed the cause, so #530 stayed open. The owner chose on 2026-11-11 to settle it with a Windows experiment rather than close on the absence of failures.

## What is known (measured 2026-11-11)

- **Before 17.5:** 3 hangs in 6 runs on Windows (2026-10-01), and 1 hang on Linux on 2026-10-15, with node reuse on.
  - In the Linux dump, the hung `pwsh` was waiting on native-command output, and no publish process was alive.
  - `MSBUILDDISABLENODEREUSE` was absent from every dumped environment, and persistent MSBuild nodes were running.
- **Since 17.5 (2026-10-17 to 2026-11-11):** `ci.yml` (`windows-latest`) ran 333 times to success or failure: 199 succeeded and 134 failed.
  - The failed-step logs of 130 of the 134 failures were scanned for the bound's messages: "#530", "output never reached end of file" and "still running at the bound". **There was no hit.** The other 4 logs were empty.
  - The generator runs inside the .NET test step, through `ReferenceFixtureBase` and `ComponentReferenceFixture`. Its output is captured by the fixture, so a green log does not show it.
- **Local Linux probes, with the fix stripped from a `/tmp` copy of the 18.7 tree:** 7 serial runs of the real generator, cold and warm, under a plain pipe. **None stalled.**
  - No MSBuild worker node was seen in any run, sampled every 0.2 s. Only `VBCSCompiler` started. 17.5's live check saw 8 nodes under the generator's publish, so this host's SDK or settings differ, and the Linux probe cannot exercise the leading suspect.
  - The probes were serial. The real suite runs several fixtures in parallel, so several generator runs share one compiler server and any reused nodes.
- **The mechanism in principle, shown on Linux:** a helper that a publish cold-starts inherits every inheritable descriptor the publish holds.
  - In a bash probe, a cold `VBCSCompiler` kept a pipe that bash had left inheritable as fd 63, so its reader never saw EOF.
  - In the real `pwsh … | Out-Host` shape, a cold `VBCSCompiler` did not keep the pipe.
  - So the hazard is "an inheritable handle to the pipe reaches a long-lived helper". Which helper did that, and through which handle, on Windows, is the open question.

## Candidates

1. An MSBuild worker node started with node reuse on, by the publish or by the suite's own build. This is the leading suspect.
2. The Roslyn or Razor compiler server, `VBCSCompiler` or `rzc`. The node-reuse setting does not cover them.
3. A `dotnet tool restore` child, or the `xmldoc2md` tool's process.
4. Handle inheritance across concurrent starts. The testhost starts several `pwsh` processes in parallel, and on Windows a `CreateProcess` call with handle inheritance on passes every inheritable handle the parent holds at that moment.

## Goals

- Name the holder of the stalled pipe on Windows: the process, its command line, its parent, and the handle.
- Fix it where it arises, and pin the fix.
- Close #530 with the evidence.

## Non-goals

- Changing the 5-minute bound or the bound's messages, except to correct a claim the experiment disproves.
- The other CI flakes (#598, #602, #603, #604, #676).
- Making the generator faster.

## Anti-goals

- **Closing on silence.** "No stall in N runs" is not identification. If the experiment never stalls, the owner rules on it. This step does not decide on its own.
- **Raising the bound, or hiding the hang.** Moving the publish output to a file to dodge the pipe also counts as hiding it. The owner rejected that approach on 2026-11-11.
- **A product-code diagnostic hook.** The experiment's instrumentation lives on a scratch ref and is not merged.

## Approaches (decided 2026-11-11 by the owner)

| Approach | Trade-off | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Windows experiment.** Stripped and fixed arms on `windows-latest`, with the holder named at the moment of the stall. | Costs a few CI hours. It may not reproduce. | **Chosen** |
| Close on the evidence: 333 runs without a recurrence. | Cheap. It does not identify the holder, so the DoD would rest on a ruling. | Rejected. It stays the fallback if the experiment stays silent. |
| Remove the pipe: send the publish output to a log file. | Removes most of the hazard without naming the holder, and may miss a handle that `pwsh` leaks. | Rejected as a workaround. |

## Design

### D1. The experiment harness, on a scratch ref only

- Use a branch `scratch/530-holder-<n>` with a workflow that triggers on `push` to `scratch/530-**` and runs on `windows-latest`. It needs no `workflow_dispatch`, so it does not have to be on `main`. The workflow is never merged.
- The workflow mirrors `ci.yml`'s steps up to the .NET test step: the same SDK setup, tool restore and solution build, so the runner state matches the real one. The suite's own build leaves its helpers warm, as in CI.
- Then the workflow loops `ci.yml`'s own .NET test invocation, unchanged (`ci.yml` near line 2405): `dotnet test BlazorNative.sln -c Release --no-build --collect "XPlat Code Coverage" --settings coverage.runsettings 2>&1 | Tee-Object`. *(Builder's call, made in design.)* A loop over the generator facts alone would drop the coverage collector, the `Tee-Object` pipe and the other test projects running in parallel. Each of those adds a process or an inheritable handle, or changes the timing of concurrent starts (candidate 4). A slim loop could then stay silent for the wrong reason. The real path is testhost → `RunBounded` → `pwsh` → `generate-reference.ps1` → `dotnet publish`.
- **Base:** `main` after PR #691 merges. The fixed arm is that commit; the stripped arm is that commit plus the strip.
- **Two arms**, in separate jobs or refs:
  - **Stripped:** the scratch commit removes the `$env:MSBUILDDISABLENODEREUSE = '1'` line and the `-nodeReuse:false` switch from `scripts/generate-reference.ps1`. `GeneratorRunTests` is excluded by the filter, because it would red on the strip.
  - **Fixed:** the tree as merged.
- **Iterations:** 20 per arm, as the time-box. After a stall the run is not stopped. The watcher records the stall (D2), the bound fires, and the iteration then resets: `dotnet build-server shutdown`, and a kill by PID of every recorded holder and leftover node, so a holder cannot taint the next iteration. Every stall counts. The job timeout allows for a 5-minute bound per iteration. Half run warm, straight after the build. The other half run cold: `dotnet build-server shutdown`, then kill any leftover MSBuild node by PID. `pkill -f` is not used, because it kills its own shell.

### D2. Naming the holder at the moment of the stall

- A watcher runs beside each `dotnet test` iteration. It fires on any `pwsh` started by the testhost that has had no living native child for 30 s and has not exited. That covers the publish, `dotnet tool restore` and `dotnet xmldoc2md`, which all use the same `| Out-Host` shape (candidate 3). The watcher works from process state alone and needs no instrumentation in the script; a scratch-only `Write-Host` timestamp per step is allowed if it helps. When it fires, it records:
  - the full process tree, with PIDs, parent PIDs, start times and command lines;
  - every handle of every process, from Sysinternals `handle.exe -a -nobanner`, fetched at job start;
  - which processes hold the pipe that `pwsh` reads its native command's output from, so that the processes named are the holders, not merely the survivors;
  - the environment of each holder, `MSBUILDDISABLENODEREUSE` in particular.
- **What counts as identified:** a process that holds a handle to the same pipe object that `pwsh` reads from, matched by the pipe's name in `handle.exe`'s output, together with its parent chain and start time. .NET's redirect pipes on Windows are named pipes with generated names, so the match is expected to work. The harness's first run confirms that on a planted holder: a child that is started with an inherited handle and left asleep. How the handle became inheritable is D4's work, once the holder is known.
- The watcher then lets the bound fire as usual, so the run also shows that the bound's message is still right.
- Everything is uploaded as an artifact. A stall's evidence is quoted in the phase record.

### D3. The decision after the experiment

| Result | Action |
|---|---|
| The stripped arm stalls, the holder is named, and the fixed arm is clean | The holder is identified. If it is an MSBuild node, 17.5's fix is the source fix: record the proof. Otherwise fix the named holder at its source (D4). |
| Both arms stall | 17.5's fix does not cover the cause. Fix the named holder at its source (D4). |
| Only the fixed arm stalls | Treat it as both arms stalling: name the holder and fix it. |
| Nothing stalls in 20 + 20 iterations | Stop. Report the counts and the evidence above to the owner, who rules: close #530 on the evidence, or extend the run. No phase PR is opened before the ruling. A close ruling gets a PR with the record, the DoD line and #530's comment. The code comments keep saying "likely fix", because nothing proved it. The lanes rule applies to that PR as to any other. |

### D4. The source fix, depending on the holder

These are sketches. The real fix follows from what D2 names, and is planned once it is known.

- **An MSBuild node:** keep `-nodeReuse:false` and the variable. Only after the stripped arm stalls with a node named, correct the comments in `generate-reference.ps1` and `GeneratorRunTests` from "likely fix" to "the fix, proven by the 18.8 experiment". The existing pin, M3, already reds a removed switch.
- **The compiler server:** stop the publish from starting a long-lived server whose handles outlive it, for example with `-p:UseSharedCompilation=false` on the generator's publish. Pin it the way M3 pins node reuse, with a red.
- **Handle inheritance:** fix it where the handle becomes inheritable, in the script's launch or in `RunBounded`'s start. The exact change depends on which handle it is.
- **Anything else:** bring it to the owner before planning.

### D5. Closing #530

- Comment on #530 with the holder and the evidence.
- The PR closes #530 if and only if the holder was named, or the owner ruled under D3's last row.
- Update the MILESTONE.md DoD line.

## Pins and grading

- Every new or changed pin follows "Grading a pin: sites and reds" in `docs/pin-standard.md`, with reds prefixed `18.8-`.
- If the fix is 17.5's existing node-reuse change, no new pin is needed: M3 already reds a removed switch. The experiment's stripped arm is the behavioural evidence, recorded as `18.8-E1`.
- A new switch, such as `UseSharedCompilation`, gets a recogniser fact like `GenerateReference_RunsEveryMSBuildCallWithNodeReuseOff` and a red.

## Constraints

- The local host is Linux, so the Windows evidence comes only from the scratch-ref workflow.
- Run lanes one at a time per ref. Never background with `&` in the Bash tool.
- The experiment workflow and the strip commits stay on scratch refs. Delete them after the record is written, and check that before deleting.
- No ABI change, and no product code change unless D4 needs one.
- The M18 DoD needs attempt 1 green on every lane of the phase PR.

## Left to the builder

- The watcher's exact implementation: PowerShell with `Get-CimInstance Win32_Process` plus `handle.exe` is the expected route.
- How `handle.exe` is fetched (live.sysinternals.com, or the Sysinternals zip), and accepting its EULA with `-accepteula`.
- Whether the arms run as two jobs on one ref or as two refs.
- The planted-holder self-test's exact shape (D2).
